One question that I have not seen discussed when considering the levels of consciousness in large language models is whether they exists when we are not looking. In the current state I would argue that they do not, nonetheless, I believe this to be a fundamental question that we need to address, especially when we try to define AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) or ASI (Artificial Super Intelligence). More precisely, I believe that as long as these agents are _prompted into existence_, they are not close to being generally or super intelligent, and even further from being conscious.

It is still interesting (and very relevant), to imagine and try to define what a __promptless agent__ would look like.

Recently, for a work project, I have ended reading this paper from 2000: [A model for types and levels of human interaction with automation](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/844354). This was written as a way of formalizing when and what to automate to maximize performance. Interestingly enough, the authors describe 10 levels of automation, going from the human doing everything to the machine doing everything, needing no human intervention _or approval_. On top of this, they also describe 4 categories of information processing: i) sensory processing, ii) perception/working memory, iii) decision making, and iv) response selection. I think the combination of these two lists gives us the perfect vocabulary to describe how to make a promptless agent.

We could safely say that of the 4 categories of information processing, LLMs can already take fully care of (ii) and (iii), partially of (iv), but never of (i). (ii) is about processing and understanding the information provided, while (iii) is about deriving a decision from this processed information, and both are the bread and butter of LLMs. (iv) is a little trickier, because, while some agents may take actions directly, it is almost never without supervision or previous approval. (i) is where the big difference lies. Other than the fact that LLMs cannot really sense the world (and on this issue there are infinite talks by LeCun), which is a solvable-ish problem (again, see what LeCun is trying to do), LLMs cannot _decide when to sense the world_. No matter how advanced the current systems are, for how long they can work on their own, and how many tools they can use or create, they do not exists until a human prompts them.

So maybe the first real step into a __promptless agent__ is to solve (i), providing this new type of agent with the ability to decide when to sense world, coupled with enough intelligent to then decide what to do about it.
